Math people

Who is Max Dehn?

  • Pioneer of geometric group theory, topology, and combinatorial group theory.
  • Studied under David Hilbert, long career in Germany before fleeing persecution in 1930’s.
  • Solved Hilbert’s third problem: showed that two polyhedra of equal volume are not necessarily equidecomposable (can’t be cut into finite pieces and reassembled to form each other).
  • One of his students was Ruth Moufang.

Here we also see Felix Klein,

  • Klein bottle
  • Erlangen Program: geometry is the study of invariants under a group of transformations
  • Vladimir Arnold (of cat map fame) used this group-theoretic perspective to unify geometry and dynamics

Arnold’s Cat Map - Vladimir

  • Russian, father studied under Emmy Noether.
  • geometry, dynamical systems, catastrophe theory, math education, et al.
  • Studied under:
    • Kolmogorov
    • Pontryagin (totally blind @ 14)
    • Alexandrov
  • Arnold’s principle:
    • “Discoveries are rarely attributed to the correct person”
  • gomboc: the first known homogeneous object with one stable and one unstable point of equilibrium, solved a problem posed by Aristotle about 2000 years ago. Arnold’s principle applies here: the gomboc was discovered by a team of researchers in 2006, but it was actually first described by Russian mathematician Vladimir Arnold in 1995.

Arnold

  • Solved Hilbert’s 13th problem at age 19. (any continuous function of three variables can be represented as a superposition of continuous functions of two variables)
  • Only a few years later his work on the problem of small denominators in dynamical systems provided the finishing touches on the long elusive explanation of the stability of the solar system (the problem for which Poincaré won the King Oscar Prize in mathematics in 1889 when he discovered chaotic dynamics ). This theory is known as KAM-theory, using the first initials of the names of Kolmogorov, Arnold and Moser.
  • Critic of Bourbaki, 1900’s trend toward higher abstraction in mathematics.
  • 1974: nominated for Field’s Medal, but then …
  • 1999: bike accident in Paris, TBI, amnesia, then full recovery.
